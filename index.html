The Basics

What Every Canadian Should Know Before Buying a Printer

A printer is a hardware device that takes digital content from a computer, smartphone, or tablet and produces a physical copy on paper or other media. Simple in concept, but the technology behind modern printers — and the choices you face when buying one — can be surprisingly complex.

In Canada, printers are among the most commonly owned pieces of office equipment, found in homes from Victoria to St. John's. Whether you're printing homework in Ontario, shipping labels for a small business in Alberta, bilingual documents in Quebec, or photographs from a trip to British Columbia's coast, choosing the right printer for the job makes a meaningful difference in your results and your long-term costs.

The challenge most Canadian buyers face isn't finding a printer — it's understanding the differences between models and technologies well enough to make a confident choice. The retail experience often involves rows of boxes with specifications that are difficult to compare without context. PrintWise exists to provide that context.

🍁 Canadian buyers should be aware that printer warranties often differ from US models. Always confirm that the model you purchase is certified for Canada and includes Canadian warranty coverage — not just a US-market warranty that may not be honoured domestically.

Printer Types

The Four Main Types Used Across Canada

Each printer type has genuine strengths for specific situations. Understanding the differences is the single most important step in choosing the right device.

💧

Inkjet Printers Most Common

Inkjet printers are the most widely sold printer type in Canadian households. They work by spraying tiny droplets of liquid ink onto paper through microscopic nozzles. The result is excellent colour reproduction, smooth gradients, and the ability to print high-quality photographs. Inkjet printers typically have a lower upfront cost, making them accessible for families and individuals who print occasionally.

The trade-off is per-page cost — ink cartridges can be expensive relative to the volume they produce, and ink can dry out in nozzles if the printer sits unused for extended periods. For Canadian households that print infrequently, a cartridge may partly dry between uses. For regular users who print colour documents and photos, inkjet remains an excellent choice.

⚡

Laser Printers Best for Offices

Laser printers use a completely different technology: a laser beam traces the image or text onto a drum coated with a photosensitive material, which then attracts powdered toner that is fused to the paper with heat. The result is sharp, fast text printing with a very low per-page cost at higher volumes.

In Canadian offices — whether in downtown Toronto, suburban Calgary, or a remote work-from-home setup in rural Nova Scotia — laser printers are the preferred choice for anyone printing primarily text documents in volume. A toner cartridge lasts considerably longer than an ink cartridge and doesn't dry out between uses. Colour laser models are available but carry a higher price. For black-and-white document printing, laser is almost universally the better long-term investment.

🖨

All-in-One Printers Most Versatile

All-in-one printers (also called multifunction printers) combine a printer with a flatbed scanner and, in most cases, a photocopier and fax capability. They're the most practical choice for Canadian home offices and small businesses that need multiple capabilities without multiple devices.

All-in-one models are available in both inkjet and laser versions, so you get to choose the core print technology that suits your needs and add scanning and copying on top. For self-employed Canadians, students, and home offices that regularly need to scan receipts, copy documents, or digitize paperwork, an all-in-one eliminates the need for a separate scanner. Most modern models connect wirelessly, allowing printing from any device on your home network.

📸

Photo Printers

Photo printers are specialized inkjet devices engineered specifically to produce photographic prints. They typically use more ink colours than a standard inkjet — sometimes six, eight, or even twelve individual ink tanks — to achieve a broader colour range and finer tonal gradations. The result is print quality that approaches or matches commercial photo lab output.

For Canadian photography enthusiasts, families who want physical prints from digital photos, or creative professionals needing high-quality output, a dedicated photo printer offers results that standard inkjet and laser models simply can't match. Some models print directly from memory cards or smartphones without requiring a computer. Compact photo printers also exist for borderless 4×6-inch or postcard-sized prints — practical for printing vacation photos or event images on the spot.

Key Features

What the Specs Actually Mean

Printer spec sheets are full of numbers that look meaningful but aren't always explained. Here's what the important ones actually tell you.

🖨

Print Resolution (DPI)

DPI stands for dots per inch — the number of individual ink dots the printer places in a square inch. Higher DPI produces sharper, more detailed output. For everyday document printing, 600 DPI is more than sufficient. For photo printing, 1200 DPI or higher produces noticeably better results. Don't let extremely high DPI numbers (4800×1200, for example) mislead you — these often refer to the maximum in one direction only and don't reflect typical print quality.

⚡

Print Speed (PPM)

PPM means pages per minute. Manufacturers' quoted PPM figures are measured under ideal conditions — typically draft-quality black text on plain paper. Real-world print speeds are often 30–50% lower than quoted figures, especially for colour printing or photo-quality output. For a household printer, 10–15 real-world PPM is perfectly adequate. For a busy Canadian office, look for a laser printer with at least 25 PPM to avoid bottlenecks.

📶

Wireless & Wi-Fi Connectivity

Most modern printers connect to your home or office Wi-Fi network, allowing any device on the same network to print without a physical cable. Look for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth support for flexible placement. Some models also support direct printing from a smartphone via app, which is particularly useful in Canadian homes where multiple family members want to print from different devices. NFC tap-to-print is available on some models for even faster mobile printing.

🔄

Automatic Duplex Printing

Duplex printing means the printer can automatically flip the page and print on both sides without you doing it manually. This is a practical feature for Canadian offices trying to reduce paper costs and environmental impact. Automatic duplex is standard on most mid-range and higher models. Manual duplex (where the printer prompts you to flip the page) is available on lower-cost models but is slower and more error-prone.

📋

Paper Tray Capacity

The standard paper tray holds 100–250 sheets on home models and 250–500+ on office models. If you print in volume — shipping labels, invoices, reports — a larger tray means less frequent reloading. Many office laser printers allow additional tray modules to be added, increasing total capacity significantly. For home use in Canada, a 150–250 sheet standard tray is generally sufficient.

🌱

Energy Efficiency (Energy Star)

Energy Star certification is a North American standard — relevant to Canadian buyers specifically — that indicates the printer meets energy efficiency guidelines set by the EPA and Natural Resources Canada. An Energy Star certified printer uses significantly less power in sleep mode and during operation. For Canadians who leave printers powered on throughout the workday, choosing an Energy Star model can noticeably reduce electricity consumption over time.

🍁 Printing in Canada

What Canadian Buyers Should Know Specifically

Printer purchasing in Canada has a few considerations that don't apply the same way in other markets. Understanding these helps you avoid common surprises after purchase.

📜

Canadian Warranty Coverage

Always verify that the printer you buy in Canada carries a Canadian warranty. Some imported or grey-market units are sold with US-only warranties that a Canadian service centre may not honour. Purchase from authorized Canadian retailers to ensure proper warranty support from coast to coast.

🇨🇦

Bilingual Documentation

Under Canadian law, consumer products sold in Canada must include instructions in both English and French. Most major printer models sold through Canadian retailers comply with this requirement. If you're purchasing online from a US retailer and shipping to Canada, verify that bilingual documentation is included.

⚡

Voltage & Power Standards

Canadian electrical outlets supply 110–120V at 60Hz — the same as the United States. Most printers sold in North America are designed for this standard. However, if purchasing an internationally-marketed model, confirm it supports Canadian power standards before buying.

📦

Canada Post Label Printing

Canadian small businesses and online sellers frequently print Canada Post shipping labels. Standard inkjet and laser printers handle Canada Post labels without issue. For high-volume label printing, a dedicated label printer may be more efficient than a standard desktop model.

🌍

Energy Star & Provincial Standards

Energy Star certification applies in Canada and is recognized by Natural Resources Canada. Several provinces, including British Columbia and Ontario, have energy efficiency incentive programs. An Energy Star printer may qualify depending on your province and current program availability.

🛒

Where to Buy in Canada

Printers are available at major Canadian electronics retailers in cities like Toronto, Vancouver, Calgary, Edmonton, Ottawa, and Montreal, as well as online through Canadian retailers. Buying from a Canadian source ensures HST/GST compliance, domestic return policies, and Canadian warranty service.

Before You Buy

Six Things to Consider Before Choosing a Printer

01

How Often Will You Print?

Infrequent printers (a few pages a week) are better served by a laser printer — ink cartridges can dry out in inkjets that sit unused. Frequent printers who need colour and photos should look at inkjet.

02

Colour or Black and White?

If 90% of your printing is text documents, a black-and-white laser printer delivers the lowest long-term cost per page. Colour inkjet covers documents and photos but at higher consumable cost.

03

Do You Need a Scanner?

If you regularly scan receipts, sign documents, or digitize paperwork, an all-in-one model adds scanning and copying without requiring a separate device or additional desk space.

04

What Is the True Cost of Ink or Toner?

The printer purchase price is only part of the cost. Calculate the cost per page using the cartridge price and rated page yield. Some entry-level printers have very expensive consumables that make them costly to run.

05

Wireless vs Wired Connection?

For households with multiple devices — phones, laptops, tablets — a Wi-Fi-enabled printer is significantly more convenient. If the printer lives next to a single desktop computer, a USB connection is simpler and never drops.

06

Available Space and Noise Level

All-in-one and laser printers tend to be physically larger and louder than compact inkjets. In a small Canadian apartment or a shared office space, noise and footprint both matter more than they do in a dedicated home office with room to spare.
